Experimental Objective:
The objective is to improve retrieval and reduce hallucinations in large-language-model-based question answering for corn cultivation by introducing Sem-RAG, a dual-store semantic retrieval framework that combines chunk-level embeddings with community-level semantic summaries.

Dataset Description:
The dataset comprises professional documents about corn planting, including agronomic knowledge, cultivation practices, and management guidelines. These documents are split into fixed-length chunks which serve as base units for retrieval. From each chunk, semantic triples (head entity, relation, tail entity) are extracted to build a local knowledge graph. The resulting corpus is used both as a text collection and as a structured graph for community detection and summary generation. A dedicated QA dataset, CornData, is constructed to evaluate the method, but the exact size and statistics are reported in the full paper rather than in the summary.

Model Description:
Sem-RAG uses a dual-store architecture. The first store is a surface semantic store that holds vector embeddings of text chunks, obtained using an embedding model. The second is a fine-grained semantic store built from community-level summaries derived from a knowledge graph. Triples extracted from documents are organised into a graph, and communities are identified via the Leiden algorithm. For each community, a large language model and additional heuristics generate thematic summaries encoding key concepts, relations, conditions, and procedural steps. Both chunk embeddings and summary embeddings are used for retrieval, and a large language model is used for answer generation conditioned on the retrieved context.

Experimental Procedure:
1. Collect and preprocess corn cultivation documents and divide them into fixed-length text chunks.
2. For each chunk, extract semantic triples representing entities and relations, and construct a local knowledge graph.
3. Apply the Leiden community detection algorithm to partition the graph into semantically coherent communities.
4. Generate structured semantic summaries for each community, capturing themes, key relations, and conditional or procedural information.
5. Encode both original chunks and community summaries into dense vector representations.
6. Implement dual-store retrieval that jointly retrieves from the chunk store and the summary store given a user question.
7. Feed the retrieved texts and summaries to a large language model to generate answers.
8. Evaluate QA performance on the CornData dataset and compare Sem-RAG against baseline retrieval strategies.

Evaluation Setup:
The evaluation compares Sem-RAG to simpler retrieval variants such as standard chunk-only RAG and graph-based retrieval methods. The system is tested using corn-knowledge QA tasks from CornData. Standard QA performance metrics (e.g., as reported in the paper) are computed on the test set to quantify answer quality and reliability. The effect of dual-store retrieval versus single-store approaches is examined.

Strengths:
- Addresses a concrete problem of retrieval quality and hallucination in domain-specific QA.
- Introduces a clear dual-store design that combines fine-grained chunk representations with higher-level community summaries.
- Leverages graph-based community detection to organise knowledge and produce interpretable summaries.
- Provides a realistic pipeline for applying LLMs to knowledge-intensive agricultural QA.

Limitations:
- The pipeline is complex and may be computationally expensive to deploy at scale.
- The method relies on accurate triple extraction and graph construction; errors in these steps may propagate through the system.
- Detailed information about the size and diversity of CornData, as well as robustness across other crops or domains, is limited in the summary.
- Human evaluation of answer usefulness and domain expert judgment are not emphasised in the brief description.
