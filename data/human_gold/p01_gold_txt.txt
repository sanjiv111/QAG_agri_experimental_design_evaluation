Experimental Objective:
The objective is to build a plant protection question-answering system that reduces hallucinations and improves factual accuracy by fine-tuning a large language model with LoRA and integrating it into a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) framework over a curated plant protection knowledge base.

Dataset Description:
The dataset is constructed from approximately 7000 professional agricultural papers and books focused on plant protection. Documents are de-duplicated using DOI, title, and abstract similarity, and further filtered with MinHash-based text similarity to remove redundant QA pairs. The text is cleaned by removing HTML tags and special characters, normalising punctuation and numerical expressions, and standardising question phrasing and dosage descriptions. Low-quality QA pairs are filtered using perplexity thresholds and length constraints, resulting in around 9000 high-quality plant protection QA pairs and an associated document corpus that forms the local knowledge base.

Model Description:
The core model is InternLM, a large Transformer-based language model designed for multilingual NLP tasks. It is adapted to plant protection QA via Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), which adds trainable low-rank matrices to selected weight matrices instead of updating all parameters, keeping fine-tuning efficient. The QA system is embedded in a RAG architecture: documents are embedded and indexed with a FAISS-based vector store, and the fine-tuned InternLM-20B-LoRA model generates answers conditioned on retrieved passages.

Experimental Procedure:
1. Collect and clean agricultural documents related to plant protection; perform de-duplication, formatting, and filtering using perplexity and length thresholds.
2. Convert selected passages into QA pairs and construct a plant protection QA dataset and a document-based local knowledge base.
3. Embed knowledge base documents into a vector space and build a FAISS index for similarity search.
4. Fine-tune InternLM with LoRA on the plant protection QA dataset to specialise it to the target domain.
5. Integrate the fine-tuned model into a RAG pipeline where a user query is embedded, relevant documents are retrieved, and the model generates answers conditioned on the retrieved context.
6. Evaluate generated answers with automatic metrics that capture semantic similarity and factual consistency.

Evaluation Setup:
The evaluation uses automatic generation metrics focused on semantic and factual alignment rather than only surface overlap. ROUGE is used to measure n-gram and structural overlap between generated and reference answers. BERTScore uses contextual embeddings to capture semantic similarity. FactCC is used to estimate factual consistency of generated text with respect to reference content. Results are reported over a test subset of the QA dataset, comparing different system variants.

Strengths:
- Uses a curated, domain-specific QA dataset and knowledge base tailored to plant protection.
- LoRA enables efficient fine-tuning of a large model without updating all parameters, which is realistic for resource-constrained settings.
- The RAG design explicitly grounds generation in retrieved expert documents, which helps mitigate hallucination.
- Evaluation metrics are chosen to emphasise semantics and factual consistency rather than just lexical overlap.

Limitations:
- Evaluation is based only on automatic metrics; there is no reported human judgment of answer usefulness or safety.
- The dataset, while high quality, is limited to plant protection and may not cover the full diversity of agricultural contexts.
- Details of baseline systems and ablation studies (e.g., LoRA vs full fine-tuning, RAG vs no-RAG) are not fully elaborated in the summary.
- Region-specific and low-resource scenarios (e.g., different languages or smallholder contexts) are not explicitly evaluated.
