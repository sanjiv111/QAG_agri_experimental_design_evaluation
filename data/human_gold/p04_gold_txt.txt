Experimental Objective:
The objective is to develop AgriGPT, a domain-specialised large language model ecosystem for agriculture that combines scalable instruction data generation, domain-adaptive pretraining, supervised fine-tuning, and multi-path retrieval (Tri-RAG), and to demonstrate that this ecosystem outperforms general-purpose LLMs on agricultural QA and reasoning tasks.

Dataset Description:
The main dataset is Agri-342K, a large agricultural instruction dataset produced by a multi-agent data engine. Agricultural knowledge is organised into nine thematic domains, and more than 600 sub-area keywords are used to collect and filter domain-relevant documents from research papers, manuals, and textbooks. Three pipelines feed the data engine: distillation of public documents, answer generation for existing QA datasets, and iterative synthetic data generation starting from a seed of human-verified instructions. A set of four collaborative LLM agents (Rethinking, Rewrite, Supervise, Evaluation) refine raw QA pairs to produce coherent, diverse, and factually grounded instructions. Additionally, AgriBench-13K is constructed as a multi-task benchmark covering different agricultural QA and reasoning tasks, with subsets like Mini-AgriBench600 and multilingual variants.

Model Description:
AgriGPT is a large language model that undergoes continual pretraining on agricultural corpora to adapt its knowledge to the domain, followed by supervised fine-tuning on Agri-342K instructions. The system integrates Tri-RAG at inference time, combining dense retrieval, sparse retrieval, and multi-hop knowledge-graph reasoning. This multi-path retrieval is designed to improve factual grounding and reasoning depth. Several model sizes are evaluated, and AgriGPT is compared to strong general-purpose LLM baselines.

Experimental Procedure:
1. Define agricultural domains and sub-areas; collect a large set of candidate documents and QA data.
2. Run the multi-agent data engine to distil, generate, and refine instructions, yielding the Agri-342K dataset.
3. Pretrain the base LLM further on agricultural corpora for domain adaptation.
4. Fine-tune the model with supervised learning on Agri-342K to align it to instruction-following and QA tasks.
5. Implement Tri-RAG, combining dense retrieval, sparse retrieval, and knowledge-graph-based multi-hop reasoning over agricultural knowledge sources.
6. Construct the AgriBench-13K benchmark and run AgriGPT and baseline LLMs on its tasks.
7. Report automatic metrics for generation quality and human-judged metrics for answer quality across different difficulty levels and tasks.

Evaluation Setup:
AgriGPT and baseline LLMs are evaluated on AgriBench-13K and its subsets. Automatic metrics include BLEU, METEOR, and ROUGE-1/2/L for lexical and structural similarity, as well as an LLM-Score that captures answer quality from an LLM judge. Human evaluations assess correctness, fluency, coherence, relevance, logical consistency, and completeness on a 1–4 scale. Results are compared across multiple baseline models, including strong general-purpose LLMs, to show the benefit of domain specialisation and Tri-RAG.

Strengths:
- Provides an end-to-end ecosystem: data engine, domain adaptation, fine-tuning, retrieval, and benchmark.
- Uses multi-agent refinement to improve the quality of instructional data and reduce hallucinations.
- Tri-RAG explicitly targets factual grounding and multi-hop reasoning, supported by both automatic and human evaluations.
- Benchmarks AgriGPT against a broad set of competitive LLMs, showing consistent performance gains.

Limitations:
- The pipeline is complex and may be difficult for other groups to reproduce without significant resources.
- The evaluation focuses on the constructed benchmark; external validation on independent agricultural datasets is less emphasised.
- Human evaluation details (e.g., number of annotators, agreement) are not fully detailed in the summary.
- The approach assumes access to substantial compute for continual pretraining and large-scale fine-tuning.
