## Evaluating the Experimental Design Quality of LLM-Generated Software Assurance Arguments
CS5063 – Evaluation of AI Systems (Group Project, 2025–26)
University of Aberdeen – MSc Artificial Intelligence

# 1. Project Overview:
Assurance arguments are structured justifications used in safety-critical software engineering, especially in domains influenced by NASA, aerospace, medical devices, and autonomous systems. A scientifically valid assurance argument must contain:
clearly stated safety or reliability claims.
structured argumentation connecting claims to evidence.
high-quality evidence (test results, analyses, verification artefacts).
rigorous experimental design components such as baselines, data, metrics, and significance evaluation.

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly being used to auto-generate assurance arguments, test plans, and verification strategies. However, recent literature identifies major risks:

LLMs fabricate evidence or produce unsupported reasoning.
LLMs exhibit truth-indifferent behaviour (“soft bullshit”).
LLM-generated SQA artefacts may violate ISO/IEC or NASA assurance principles.

# 2. Goal of this Project:
We evaluate whether LLM-generated assurance arguments demonstrate valid experimental design, focusing on:

Baselines.
Data & evidence sources.
Metrics & evaluation measures.
Significance assessments.
Traceability & structure (Claim → Argument → Evidence).

# 3. Research Questions
RQ1: Do LLMs correctly generate the essential components of experimental design—baselines, data, metrics, significance tests—within software assurance arguments?

RQ3: Do LLM-produced assurance arguments conform to software assurance standards such as ISO/IEC 12207, ISO/IEC 25010, and NASA SQA principles?
