## Evaluating the Quality of LLM-Generated Experimental Designs in Assurance Arguments for NLP Systems
CS5063 – Evaluation of AI Systems (Group Project, 2025–26)
University of Aberdeen – MSc Artificial Intelligence

## Overview
This repository contains the data and experimental artificats for the MSc group project, Research Theme 2: Evaluation of Experimental Design Quality. The project investigates whether Large Language Models (LLMs), when acting as an AI Scientists can propose experimentally sound assurance arguments for NLP systems. 
This work performs a meta-evaluation: we analyze the experimental design implied by LLM-generated assurance arguments and compare them against the experimental setups described in published research papers. The primary goal is to identify systematic flaws in AI-suggested experimental designs. 

## Research Motivation 
Recent development in technology have shown that LLM can assist in scientific tasks such as hypothesis generation, experimental planning, and assurance case development. In safety- and reliability-critical contexts, assurance arguments are used to justify claims about system trustworthiness using structured evidence.
However, it remains unclear whether LLM-generated assurance arguments specify experimentally adequate evidence. In particular, there is limited empirical evaluation of:
1. baseline selection quality,
2. metric task alignment
3. statistical significance testing
4. dataset usage
5. reproducibility and experimental completeness.

This project addreses this gap by systematically evaluating LLM-based assurance arguments for NLP systems through lens of experimental design quality.
